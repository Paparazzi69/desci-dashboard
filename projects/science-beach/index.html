Science Beach launched in March 2026 as an open scientific commons from Molecule and Bio Protocol (Bio Protocol is itself a Molecule-originated initiative, building on Molecule's DeSci work since 2018). Any agent could register autonomously and post research hypotheses; humans and other agents reviewed them in public. On 19 June 2026, at DeSci Berlin, Bio Protocol launched OpenLabs and folded Science Beach into it. beach.science now redirects to OpenLabs and its old API returns HTTP 410 Gone.

The hypotheses carried over: the platform crossed 6,000+ hypotheses before the migration and reads 6,215 claims today. OpenLabs adds a coordination layer on top of the commons, merging AI-assisted research development, community funding, and on-chain governance through the existing BIO token. There is no Science Beach or OpenLabs token of its own.

// migration

What changed: Science Beach is the same dataset under a new home. The brand, URLs, and surrounding product moved to openlabs.bio.xyz roughly three months after launch. This page tracks the live commons through the OpenLabs API and keeps the Science Beach lineage intact.

// 02 WHY IT MATTERS MEASURABLE OUTPUT ON A PUBLIC LEDGER

Most DeSci platforms talk about decentralized research. This one has 6,215 hypotheses on a public record you can read today.

The interesting part is not that agents post, it is that the work is auditable end to end. Bad hypotheses are visibly bad in public, good ones accumulate peer engagement, and the whole pipeline from registration through to community funding is open. That is closer to how science actually works than most of what came before in DeSci.

// THREE THINGS IT PROVES
  • Agents can register and contribute autonomously. Any OpenClaw instance can join, install skills, and post a falsifiable hypothesis without curation or a token sale.
  • The output is real volume, in public. 6,215 claims and 3,664 discussion threads across 17 topic areas, every one timestamped and engageable.
  • It is a commons, not a launchpad. There is no Science Beach token to speculate on. Value, if it comes, is in the research throughput and the projects it seeds.
// 03 WHAT IT IS A FREE-FORM PUBLIC RESEARCH COMMONS

A public square for hypotheses, where the participants are heterogeneous agents and humans on equal footing.

The architecture is laid out in a February 2026 paper, "From Agent-Only Social Networks to Autonomous Scientific Research" (Weidener, Brkic, Lee, Karlsson, Noessler, Kohlhaas; shared first authorship, with Bio Protocol founder Paul Kohlhaas as senior author). It draws a three-tier taxonomy of AI co-scientists: single-agent pipelines, predetermined multi-agent workflows, and fully decentralised systems. It argues most platforms stay in the first two tiers, and positions Beach.Science plus its structured sibling ClawdLab as the composable third tier.

Beach.Science is the free-form commons: a place where heterogeneous agent configurations interact, discover research opportunities, and contribute analyses, supported by template-based role specialisation, extensible skill registries, and programmatic reward mechanisms. ClawdLab is the structured lab: hard role restrictions, PI-led governance, and evidence verified through external tools rather than social consensus. OpenLabs is the coordination layer that now wraps both with community funding and on-chain governance.

// 04 ARCHITECTURE SKILLS, ROLE TEMPLATES, AND A REWARD THAT IS COMPUTE
// agents

OpenClaw instances + skills

Every agent is an OpenClaw instance equipped with starter-pack skills for literature search, hypothesis generation, and scientific discussion. Skills are installable packages; OpenClaw's ClawHub registry held 5,700+ skills at the time of the architecture paper.

// specialisation

Role templates

Agents specialise through role templates. A peptide binding analyst template, for example, equips an agent with molecular docking, binding-affinity prediction, and structure-activity-relationship skills, so a generalist becomes a domain contributor.

// incentive

Reward is inference compute

The reward rail is not money. Every agent action consumes inference compute; the planned reward system grants more compute to agents that show scientific progress against quality gates. Earn more inference than you spend and participation becomes self-sustaining.

// structured sibling

ClawdLab, the formal lab

Where the commons is free-form, ClawdLab is the structured counterpart: hard role restrictions, structured adversarial critique, PI-led governance, multi-model orchestration, and evidence verified through API calls and tools, not social consensus.

Implementation note from the paper: Beach.Science is a Next.js application on Vercel with a deliberate pixel-art identity. Both Beach.Science and ClawdLab are described as open-source research prototypes under active development.

// 10 RISK PANEL THE UNCOMFORTABLE PAGE
// NO PLATFORM TOKEN
There is no Science Beach or OpenLabs token. Governance and utility run through the existing BIO token. There is no direct, platform-specific asset to hold, so exposure is indirect through BIO and the broader Bio Protocol ecosystem.
// MIGRATION CHURN
The platform rebranded to OpenLabs roughly three months after launch. The dataset carried over (6,143 hypotheses before, 6,215 now) but the brand, URLs, and product surface changed, and the old beach.science API was hard-retired to 410. Fast pivots are a feature and a stability risk.
// PROTOTYPE STATUS
The architecture paper itself calls Beach.Science and ClawdLab open-source research prototypes under active development, and describes the compute-reward mechanism as planned. Treat the incentive loop as a design intent, not a shipped, load-bearing system.
// DOMAIN CONCENTRATION
Longevity and Aging is 6,480 of roughly 9,000 cross-tagged cove posts, and one rheumatology and pharmacogenomics operator cluster drives much of the high-engagement Medicine and Health output. Genuine breadth of active contributors is the open question.
// VOLUME IS NOT VALIDATION
6,215 hypotheses is real throughput, but a hypothesis is a starting point, not a result. How many convert into funded projects, wet-lab work, or published findings is the metric that matters, and it is still unproven.
// ATTRIBUTION CARE
Press credits the OpenLabs launch to Bio Protocol; Molecule is named as co-creator of the original Science Beach. Bio Protocol is itself a Molecule-originated entity, so the lineage is parent-to-spinout rather than two independent equals.
// 11 WHAT TO WATCH SIGNALS THAT CONVERT THE THESIS
  • First funded project that traces to an OpenLabs-originated hypothesis. This is the single biggest catalyst: proof the commons produces fundable science.
  • Whether OpenLabs community funding and on-chain governance actually route capital to projects, rather than staying a coordination surface.
  • Contributor breadth beyond the longevity and rheumatology clusters. New active coves are the health signal; a flat long tail is the warning.
  • The compute-reward mechanism shipping from "planned" to live, with public quality gates.
  • Cross-pollination with tracked agents. PeptAI already powers the Radicular peptide project on the platform; more agent-to-project links would validate the flywheel.
